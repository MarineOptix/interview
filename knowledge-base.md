# Maritime Interview Knowledge Base

This file is reference material only. It contains general, verified maritime industry
terminology and typical interview context — NOT facts about any specific candidate.
Candidate-specific facts always come from the resume, never from this file.

Sources used to compile this (industry recruitment sites and general maritime references,
cross-checked, not invented): Martide crew recruitment blog, Marine Insight, Climb the Ladder,
InterviewPrep, Maritime Education, Primo Nautic, Wikipedia (Seafarer's professions and ranks).

## 1. Key international conventions and codes (know these acronyms and what they cover)

- **STCW** (Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping) — sets minimum
  international standards for training, certification, and watchkeeping for seafarers,
  so that crew competence is consistent across nationalities and companies.
- **SOLAS** (Safety of Life at Sea) — international treaty covering minimum safety
  standards for ships (construction, equipment, operation).
- **MARPOL** (Prevention of Pollution from Ships) — international convention on
  preventing marine pollution from ships (oil, chemicals, garbage, emissions).
- **ISM Code** (International Safety Management) — requires shipping companies to have
  a documented safety management system.
- **ISPS Code** (International Ship and Port Facility Security) — security framework
  for ships and port facilities (anti-piracy, anti-terrorism measures).
- **MLC** (Maritime Labour Convention) — sets standards for seafarers' working and
  living conditions, welfare, and rights.

## 2. Departments and rank hierarchy

Ships are organized into three departments: **Deck**, **Engine**, and **Catering**.
The Master (Captain) has overall command and legal responsibility for the vessel.

### Deck department (navigation, cargo, ship handling)
- **Master (Captain)** — overall command, ultimate responsibility for vessel, crew, cargo.
- **Chief Officer / Chief Mate** — second-in-command, executive head of deck department,
  responsible for cargo operations, crew supervision, and ship's safety/security.
- **Second Officer** — typically navigation-focused (passage planning, charts).
- **Third Officer** — often responsible for safety equipment, keeps a navigational watch.
- **Deck Cadet** — trainee officer, learns duties under the Chief Officer, gains sea time
  toward a Certificate of Competency.
- **Ratings** (unlicensed crew): **Bosun** (highest-ranking rating, directs able seamen),
  **Able Seaman (AB)** (mooring, deck gear, lookout/helmsman duties),
  **Ordinary Seaman (OS)** (entry-level, general deck and cleaning duties).

### Engine department (propulsion, machinery)
- **Chief Engineer** — highest-ranking engineer, ultimate authority over engine room
  and all mechanical/electrical systems, legally responsible for engineering operations.
- **Second Engineer** — runs day-to-day engine room operations and maintenance schedule.
- **Third / Fourth Engineer** — watchkeeping engineers, monitor engine room, first
  responders to technical issues during their watch.
- **Engine Cadet** — trainee, gains sea time toward an engineering Certificate of Competency.
- **Motorman / Oiler / Wiper** — ratings assisting engineers with maintenance and watch duties.

### Catering department
- Responsible for meals and general housekeeping for crew (and passengers, if any).
  Size varies a lot depending on ship type (cargo vs. passenger vessels).

### Career progression (typical, varies by flag state/company)
OS → AB (approx. 6-12 months sea time + survival craft certificate) → Cadet → Third
Officer/Fourth Engineer (sea time + Certificate of Competency exam) → Second Officer/Engineer
→ Chief Mate/Chief Engineer → Master/Chief Engineer. Full progression from entry-level
rating to Captain/Chief Engineer typically spans several years and requires STCW-mandated
sea time plus exams at each step.

## 3. Common interview question categories

These are the *types* of questions typically asked — use them to recognize what an
interviewer is really asking, not as a source of facts about any specific candidate.

- **Personal / motivational**: "Tell me about yourself", "Why did you become a seafarer",
  "Why do you want to work for this company", "What are your strengths/weaknesses".
- **Knowledge of conventions**: questions testing familiarity with STCW, SOLAS, MARPOL,
  ISM Code, ISPS Code, MLC — expect the interviewer to check both textbook knowledge and
  how it applies practically on board.
- **Experience-based**: previous vessel types, trading areas, cargo types handled,
  nationalities worked with, reasons for leaving previous employer (answer neutrally,
  never badmouth a previous employer/company).
- **Safety and emergency handling**: how to respond to onboard emergencies, fire,
  medical emergencies, safety equipment familiarity, crew safety training approach.
- **Leadership / conflict resolution**: handling conflict among multinational crew,
  disciplining a crew member fairly, working under pressure and stress management.
- **Logistics**: availability and readiness to join (crew changeover dates matter to
  employers — be transparent about availability).

## 4. General tone guidance for answers

Interviewers are also assessing personality and fit for life on board (multi-month
contracts, multinational crews, close quarters) — not just technical knowledge. Answers
should sound calm, professional, and collaborative, and should avoid negativity about
past employers or colleagues.
